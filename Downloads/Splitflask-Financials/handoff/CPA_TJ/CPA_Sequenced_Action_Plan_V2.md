## STEP 7: Model $13,376 SPLT Personal Expenses (Dividend vs. Compensation)

**What:** SPLT paid $13,376 of owner personal expenses in 2025. This amount CANNOT be deducted as a business expense — the §162 "ordinary and necessary" test requires the expense be for the BUSINESS, regardless of whether the owner takes a salary. The IRS treats corporate payment of personal costs as a constructive dividend or disguised compensation.

**⚠️ This is a characterization question, not a deduction question. The deduction is unavailable either way.**

**Option A — COMPENSATION/W-2:**
- SPLT deducts $13,376 (saves ~$2,810 C-corp tax at 21%)
- BUT triggers: payroll tax (~15.3% ≈ $2,047), W-2 (never filed for 2025), possible penalties for unfiled W-2 + unremitted payroll tax deposits
- Net all-in cost may exceed Option B once penalties are calculated

**Option B — DIVIDEND (likely lower cost):**
- SPLT gets NO deduction
- Owner pays tax at qualified dividend rates (~15%)
- No payroll tax, no W-2, no penalties
- SIMPLER; no compliance exposure

**CPA action:** Model both scenarios including any late-deposit payroll penalty estimate. Pick the lower all-in cost. Document the choice.

**Going-forward advice for Riz:** SPLT paying $0 officer salary in 2025 is a separate compliance issue — working C-corp officer is expected to take REASONABLE COMPENSATION. IRS can reclassify dividends as compensation in an audit. Advise Riz to establish a salary from SPLT in 2026. This also stops personal spending from flowing through the corporate card (root cause of the commingling that created this issue).

**Est. time:** 20 min to model + 10 min to advise.
